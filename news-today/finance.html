Indian Rupee Hits New All-Time Low of 96.20 Against US Dollar — Down 5.5% Since Middle East Crisis Began

The rupee closed at 96.20 against the US dollar on Monday (May 18), hitting an all-time low. The figure is 0.2% lower than the previous close. The drop comes at a time when the Middle East crisis has created a sense of instability in markets. With this, the currency has plummeted by 5.5% since the crisis began. Last week, the rupee breached the Rs 96 for a dollar threshold for the first time in history, hitting an intraday low of 96.14 on Friday before settling at 94.97.

The rising price of oil is one of the biggest reasons behind the fall of the rupee. Brent Crude rose to $111 per barrel on Monday amid reports of an attack on a nuclear power plant in the UAE. Reports also state that US President Donald Trump is considering potential military options against Iran in the latest round of discussions.

Ponmudi R, CEO of a financial advisory company, told the Times of India that the rupee's fall has created unease among investors regarding India's "worsening inflation trajectory" and "rising import bill." He also noted that the slowdown comes at a point when the macroeconomic environment is under "severe strain."

The government has already started taking steps to curb the rupee's fall. Restrictions on the import of precious metals represent the biggest initiative on this front. The Reserve Bank of India has also intervened by tightening the rules around the opening positions of banks. Despite these measures, currency traders expect the rupee to continue falling throughout the week. If the RBI's steps are successful, the fall will be gradual — otherwise, steep and rapid losses may be on the cards.
