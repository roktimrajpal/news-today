WHO Declares International Emergency Following Ebola Outbreak in Uganda and DRC

The World Health Organisation has declared a global health emergency following an Ebola outbreak connected to the Bunibugyo strain of the virus in Uganda and the Democratic Republic of Congo. As of May 16, there are eight confirmed cases, 246 suspected infections, and 80 deaths linked to the virus in parts of the DRC. Cases have also been reported in Kampala, Uganda's capital, and Kinshasa, raising fears of the virus spreading beyond remote areas before authorities can contain it. The WHO also highlighted that the outbreak could be "much larger" than what has been reported and detected so far.

The outbreak is particularly dangerous as it is caused by the Bunibugyo strain — a rare variant that most standard field tests fail to detect and for which no vaccine currently exists. It has also caused infections inside hospitals and clinics. Early symptoms resemble flu and malaria, including weakness and body pain, making early detection difficult.

Despite this, health experts say there is no need to panic. Unlike Covid-19, Ebola does not spread through the air or through casual contact. The outbreak does not spell immediate trouble for India — the country already has a rigorous screening mechanism at airports that tests visitors from affected countries. Health experts also see no need to shut borders. That said, the outbreak serves as a stark reminder of how a threat in one country can spread rapidly in today's hyper-connected world.
